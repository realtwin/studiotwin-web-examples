Stillwater — built with Opus + StudioTwin MCP

Production journal

The story, expanded

The site tells this in eleven short beats. Here is the long version.

00 · Stillwater. The brief was an 18-second capture of a blue-hour swamp: warm mauve sky, cold teal fog on black mirror water, bald cypresses and a small white boat with a lit cabin. The goal was to rebuild that mood as a real-time world whose assets all come from StudioTwin, and then hand the boat to the viewer.

01 · Concept. Before any geometry existed, the agent broke down what made the reference work and asked ConceptLab for a key frame. It then refined that frame with an edit on the same Concept: dusty mauve dusk, sky bleeding into the fog. Sky colour, fog tint and grade were all checked against this frame.

02 · Environment. The sky came straight from a written brief. text-to-environment-map painted the whole 360° of soft, creamy mauve cloud in one pass; a ConceptLab edit filled the empty upper sky with high cloud, merged back only where it was needed; and environment-map-upscale-cpu lifted the panorama to 16384×8192 HDR. The dome shows it at 8K on desktop and 4K on phones, with a blue-hour split-tone grade in the sky shader: indigo shadows, dusty-rose cloud tops. The 2K HDR also lights every surface and fills every reflection. The moon is drawn in-engine, low over the channel.

03 · Trees. Trees are the worst subject for single-image 3D reconstruction: thin limbs, holes of sky and thousands of leaves. So the forest is constructed. - Growth. A species table (bald cypress, water tupelo, bleached snag) drives a recursive grower in code. It builds the flared buttress, crooked limbs, flat broken crowns, and knees clustered along the root lines. - Dressing. StudioTwin supplies the surfaces: a text-to-material cypress bark, plus ConceptLab foliage sprays and Spanish-moss strands keyed into alpha cards. - Motion. Travelling gusts bend trunks, branches and sprays, and the moss swings and lifts like cloth. - Scale. Twelve grown variants fill the banks, each in three levels of detail. The branch skeleton comes from one seeded stream that no detail setting touches, so a tree seen from far away has exactly the limbs it will have up close. Distance only decimates: fewer tube sides and rings, fewer and broader foliage cards that dissolve into the fog. Bands switch with hysteresis, so nothing pops.

04 · Materials. Mud, bark, lily pads and teak decking each started as one sentence to text-to-material, which returned seamless PBR sets.

05 · No. 86. The hero boat is a ConceptLab object concept turned into a 54k-face Tripo mesh with 4K PBR. Blender measurements located the bow, the waterline and the cabin volume, so the cabin windows could be lit from inside by a shader.

06 · Heron Bend. The great blue heron went from concept to mesh, then through tripo-auto-rig with an avian skeleton (76 bones). Its skin weights were cleaned up in Blender, where two actions were keyed: an idle (breathing, scanning, a head tilt, a feather ruffle) and a strike. The bend is lived in, too: - Buildings. A stilt cabin and a fishing shack glow from inside. - Wind. Lanterns and a hanging HERON BEND sign swing in the same wind as the trees. - Water. Each fish is its own agent. It chases a slot that slides from the bow to the flanks, sprints when it falls behind, peels off to investigate and races back, keeps clear of the hull and banks into its turns. Now and then one leaps. - Air. Birds are dusk silhouettes built and flown in code: a flock of swifts (boids with flap bursts, glides and banking) and, now and then, a line of waders with slow wingbeats, necks tucked and legs trailing. Fireflies drift low over the banks and the open water, each breathing and flashing on its own rhythm.

The cabin, shack, sign, lanterns and fish are ConceptLab concepts turned into Tripo meshes.

07 · The bird alone. For a few seconds every overlay fades and the camera settles on the heron's profile while it hunts.

08 · Sound. These are all StudioTwin text-to-sound-effect clips: - frogs, crickets and a far owl - the outboard - the heron's croak - a sign creaking on its chains - a fish splash - wingbeats

Sound unlocks on the first tap, so it works on phones as well as desktops.

09 · The ledger. Every generation and credit, pulled live from the job list. USD is at StudioTwin's rate of 50 credits per dollar (CREDITS_PER_DOLLAR in the StudioTwin API).

10 · Made with StudioTwin MCP. The end card sends people to studiotwin.ai/mcp, or hands them the boat. Take the helm: there's something yellow bobbing just ahead.

Built by an AI agent (Claude Opus) driving StudioTwin MCP end to end.

Segments

Reference

  • Source: a 17.9 s, 60 fps capture.
  • What makes the look:
  • a two-layer atmosphere: a warm mauve sky over a teal height fog lying on the water
  • near-black mirror water with moonlight on it
  • a bald-cypress tunnel with buttresses, flat crowns, moss and knees
  • a white cabin boat, "86", with amber windows
  • a teal/magenta grade with bloom and vignette

Trees (constructed)

  • Grower (trees.js). Per-species parameters (trunk flare, lean, limb angles, crown shape, knee count), a recursive branch walk, and swept tubes with bark UVs.
  • Cypress: flat foliage pads in the top zone.
  • Tupelo: a swollen base and an open crown.
  • Snag: bleached, broken and leafless.
  • Dressing.
  • Bark is StudioTwin text-to-material, darkening and wetting toward the waterline in the shader.
  • Foliage and moss are ConceptLab card concepts, keyed to clean alpha and cut out with a screen-space sharpened edge.
  • Wind (TSL).
  • Each vertex carries its height and branch-level weights.
  • Travelling gust bands sweep across the swamp. Trunks sway slowly, limbs faster and sprays fastest.
  • Moss strands swing and lift like cloth.
  • Placement.
  • Twelve variants (6 cypress, 3 tupelo, 3 snag), each in near, mid and far detail from one shared skeleton, are instanced across the banks.
  • Trees at the water's edge and along the camera path are kept low so the sky over the channel stays open.
  • A sight corridor keeps the moon clear in the channel shot.
  • The whole forest is grown at load in about a quarter of a second.

Every mesh passed through a scripted Blender step: normalise, orient, decimate, Draco. The orientation of each prop was read from orthographic renders along its axes, and the placement code uses those readings.

Materials

text-to-material produced mud bank, cypress bark ×2, teak deck and lily/duckweed, each as tileable 1024² PBR. The terrain uses the mud as a node material that turns dark and glossy at the waterline.

Lily pads are single objects, so they got a single-object pipeline: the pad mesh's UV layout became a guide image, a ConceptLab edit painted a pad onto it, and image-to-material turned that into a full PBR set that lands exactly on the pad's UVs. The Spanish moss went the same way: a ConceptLab edit turned the strand card dark, wet and slimy, keyed back to clean alpha.

Animation

  • Heron: idle and strike actions.
  • Boat: bob and roll.
  • Wind-driven motion: trees, moss, lanterns and the sign all share one gust field.
  • Wildlife: fish chasing the boat and leaping, a swift flock, passing waders, fireflies.
  • Story camera: each chapter is a held shot in the boat's frame and keeps drifting; a lens shift keeps the subject clear of the text.
  • Play mode: the camera blends into a chase cam with collisions.

Sound

text-to-sound-effect supplied these clips: - Loops: ambience, outboard - One-shots: heron call, reveal swell, squeak, creak, splash, wings

The outboard's pitch follows the throttle. The creak's volume follows the camera's distance from the sign and lanterns, and each splash is timed to a fish leap.

Engine

  • Stack: three.js r186 on WebGPURenderer, with an automatic WebGL2 fallback. Shading and post are written as TSL node materials, with no hand-written GLSL.
  • Post pipeline: a 4× MSAA scene pass with alpha-to-coverage on foliage and moss, NaN/HDR sanitise, a soft anamorphic-style lens (highlight streaks, halation and a de-speckle pass that calms sub-pixel sparkle), bloom, a split-tone grade with vignette, then SMAA on the graded frame. The story camera follows a smoothed scroll value so touch scrolling glides.
  • Performance: a Mobile tier (fewer mid/far trees, shorter detail ranges, fewer fish and birds, 4K sky), adaptive resolution, and a Max tier.
  • Loading: a staged progress bar that tracks downloads, world build, per-object shader compile and first frame, so it never claims 100 % early.
  • Build: Vite, Lenis scroll.

What to take away

  • ConceptLab is the art-direction spine. Every mesh, card and sky decision traces back to an approved frame, and an edit on the same Concept keeps composition while fixing what's wrong.
  • Use image-to-3D where it shines: solid objects. Boats, buildings, signs, birds and fish reconstruct well. For trees, combine StudioTwin surfaces (materials and cards) with procedural growth.
  • Tripo meshes are production-usable after a scripted Blender pass. The boat's atlas even kept the "86".
  • Render, critique, patch, re-render. Every frame was rendered headless, compared with a vision model against the reference, fixed and rendered again.
