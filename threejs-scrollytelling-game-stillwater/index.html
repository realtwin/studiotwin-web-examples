Stillwater

A blue-hour cypress swamp, made from nothing but prompts. Claude Opus directed it end to end through StudioTwin MCP: the concepts, a 16K sky, the meshes, the materials and the sound. The trees, the water and the wildlife are grown and animated in code.

01 · Concept

It began with a single frame.

The agent watched an 18-second capture of a swamp and wrote down what made it work: warm mauve dusk above, cold teal fog below, a mirror of black water. Then it asked ConceptLab to paint it.

An edit on the same Concept set the palette: dusty mauve dusk bleeding into the fog. That frame became the art direction for everything that followed.

02 · Environment

A painted sky, wrapped all the way around.

The mauve clouds came straight from a written brief: text-to-environment-map painted the whole 360° sky in one pass, a ConceptLab edit added soft high cloud to the empty upper sky, and environment-map-upscale-cpu took the result to 16K.

The sky shows at 8K on desktop and 4K on phones, graded to blue hour in the shader. The same panorama lights every surface and fills the reflections on the water. The moon is drawn in-engine, low over the channel.

03 · Trees

The trees were grown, not scanned.

Thin limbs and a thousand leaves are the hardest thing to reconstruct from one image. So the forest is constructed: a grower builds bald cypress, water tupelo and bleached snags, limb by limb, with flared buttresses and knees along the roots.

StudioTwin dresses them. The bark is a text-to-material set, and the foliage sprays and Spanish moss are ConceptLab cards. Every spray and strand moves in the same wind, and the moss swings like cloth.

Distance never swaps in a different tree. Each one is grown once and thinned for range: the same limbs with fewer sides, and fewer, broader sprays that soften into the fog.

04 · Materials

Surfaces, not guesses.

Mud banks, cypress bark and teak decking all started as a sentence. text-to-material returned seamless PBR sets: albedo, normal, roughness and height.

Lily pads are objects, not surfaces, so a tiling wall of pads was the wrong tool. The agent drew a guide from the pad mesh's own UV layout, with the outline, notch and veins where they land. ConceptLab painted a real pad over that guide, the agent fitted it back onto the UVs, and image-to-material turned it into a single-pad PBR set.

05 · Hero asset

Meet No. 86.

A small pilothouse boat: white hull, navy boot stripe, warm light in the cabin. ConceptLab drew it on a neutral backdrop, and Tripo v3.1 detailed turned that into a 54k-face mesh with 4K PBR.

The agent measured the hull in Blender to find the bow, the waterline and the cabin volume. It then lit the cabin windows from inside with a shader.

06 · Wildlife

Heron Bend.

Every swamp needs a resident. The great blue heron went from concept to mesh, then through tripo-auto-rig for a bird skeleton. The agent repaired the skin weights in Blender and animated it there: it breathes, scans the water and strikes.

The bend is lived in. A stilt cabin and a fishing shack glow from inside, and lanterns and the HERON BEND sign swing in the breeze; each started as a ConceptLab concept. Fish chase the boat, dart ahead and peel away. Swifts wheel overhead as a flock, and now and then a line of waders crosses the channel. Fireflies drift over the water, and the surface is never still: slow swell, gusts, and rings where something touches it.

07 · Sound

You can hear it, too.

Frogs, crickets, a far-off owl, the putter of an outboard, a heron's squawk, a sign creaking on its chains, a fish splash and wingbeats. Each one came from a single line of text through text-to-sound-effect, as seamless loops where needed.
